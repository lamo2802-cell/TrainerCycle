How to use TrainerCycle

A quick walkthrough of everything the app can do, in the order you'd actually use it.

Setting your FTP

Everything in TrainerCycle is built around your FTP (Functional Threshold Power) — it's what every %FTP target in every workout is calculated from.

  1. Open Settings and find the FTP box.
  2. Enter your number and hit Save. It's saved to your account, so it applies everywhere — Live Ride, Plans, the library's Intensity Factor numbers, all of it.

There's also a Weight box just below it — enter your weight in kg to see your power-to-weight ratio (W/kg), a common fitness benchmark. It's shown as a static reference in Settings, and live next to your watts number while riding.

Don't know your FTP? Run the Ramp Test from the library (see Riding Live below) — it suggests a new FTP automatically based on your best minute of power, and shows whether it's an increase or decrease (with the percentage) before you accept it. FTP shifts as your fitness changes, so it's worth retesting every 6-8 weeks or so — if you're following a plan that doesn't end with a built-in test, we'll prompt you to retest once it's complete, before suggesting what to do next.

Building a workout

Hit Create Workout on the Workouts page to build your own structured workout from scratch, on its own dedicated page.

Add a single step

  1. Set a duration (mm:ss), a target type (% FTP, Watts, or % HRmax), and a low/high range.
  2. Click Add step. It appears in the step list below, colored by intensity zone.

Add an interval set

For repeated work/recovery blocks (e.g. 4x8 minutes), use the Add interval set form instead — set the work duration/target, recovery duration/target, and how many repetitions, and it builds the whole block in one go.

Save it

Give it a name and click Save to library. It's now yours, synced to your account, and available from every page's workout picker — but only visible to you, not other users.

Using the library

Every workout you've built, plus a large set of prebuilt ones (Recovery through VO2 Max, Threshold, Over/Unders, Anaerobic, and the FTP Test) live in the library on the Workouts page.

  • Filter by type, or choose "Custom (mine)" to see only what you've built.
  • Sort by duration or Intensity Factor, either direction.
  • Click any workout row to open a full preview — its complete profile and step list — with a button to load it straight into Live Ride.
  • Every entry shows its duration and IF (Intensity Factor) — calculated properly from normalized power, so hard intervals push the number up the way they do on Strava or TrainerRoad.

Following a plan

The Plans page groups plans into four phases — Foundation (new to training, limited time, or coming back from something), Base (building your aerobic engine), Build (sweet spot work raising sustainable power), and Specialty (threshold/VO2 sharpening) — shown as a roadmap at the top of the page so it's clear how they connect before you pick one. New to structured training? Start with 8-Week Beginner Foundation — it's gentle, and includes built-in deload weeks (lighter, easier weeks marked right on the plan) so you actually recover as you build. Use the rides/week filter to find one that fits how much time you've actually got, from 2 rides a week up to 5.

Click a plan to expand its week-by-week breakdown, including rest days. Click any day's workout name and it jumps straight into Live Ride or TrainerStrength (whichever fits that day) with the session loaded and ready to go.

Hit Follow Plan on one you want to commit to — it aligns to the coming Monday and shows a banner at the top of the page with your current week and next scheduled session, one tap away. Not feeling that particular session? Skip moves you on to the next one without losing your place in the plan. Finish a plan and, where there's a natural next step, we'll suggest it directly — no need to go hunting for what to do next.

Building your own plan

The Plan builder (Training → Plan builder) makes a plan exactly how you want it, week by week. Start from scratch, copy one of the TrainerCycle plans, or pick Triathlon or duathlon.

  • Tap any day to set it: a ride from the workout library, a run (easy, long, strides, tempo, intervals or hills, for however many minutes), a swim (easy, technique, endurance, CSS intervals, speed, open water or a CSS test, for however many metres), a brick (a ride followed straight away by a run) or a strength session. Tick Same on this day in every week to fill a whole column at once.
  • Each week has buttons to mark it as a deload, copy it into the next week, duplicate it or delete it.
  • Save and follow makes it your plan: it shows on Today and the Calendar like any other, and rides, runs and swims you log tick its sessions off. Your plans also appear at the top of Plans with an Edit link.

Triathlon and duathlon plans are built around your race: sprint, Olympic or 70.3 triathlon, or a sprint or standard duathlon. Pick the race date, the days you can train and your experience, and you get base, build and peak weeks with a lighter week every fourth week, bricks on the long ride day, then a taper and race week. The race shows a countdown on Today. You can change any session before saving it.

Logging swims

Log a swim from the Ride button (Log a swim), from Today, or from Rides: enter the time, distance, pool or open water and how it felt. If Strava is connected, swims come in by themselves.

Swims count towards your Fitness, Fatigue and Form. Swim TSS uses your CSS (critical swim speed), the swimming version of FTP, which you set in Settings from a 400 m and a 200 m time trial. It also sets the paces in plan swim sessions.

Notes, logged activities and single workouts

On the Calendar, click or tap any day (or use + Add) for three options:

  • Add a note on any day, such as how you slept or a kit change. Notes show on the day; tap one to change or delete it.
  • Log an activity on today or an earlier day: a ride, run or swim you did away from TrainerCycle. Enter the time, an optional distance (and average power for a ride) and how it felt. It counts towards your Fitness, Fatigue and Form.
  • Plan a workout on today or a later day: a ride from the library, a run, a swim or a strength session. You don't need to be following a plan. It shows on Today on that day, and a ride, run or swim of the same sport that day ticks it off. Drag it to another day to move it, or tap it to change or delete it.

Riding live

Connect your gear

  1. Open Live Ride in Chrome or Edge (Safari/iOS doesn't support the Bluetooth this needs).
  2. Click Connect trainer and pick your trainer from the browser's device list.
  3. Optionally click Connect HR strap too.
A trainer connection is required before you can start a ride — this stops you from starting a session that won't actually control resistance.

Pick a workout and start

Choose a workout from the menu (or "Free ride" if you just want to monitor without a script), then hit Start. The overview chart shows your whole ride's planned intensity with your live power/HR traced on top; the zoomed chart below it shows just the current step in detail. Your watts number changes color live based on which intensity zone you're in relative to your FTP, and if you've entered a weight in Settings, your live W/kg shows right alongside it.

While riding

  • Bail to recovery — drops the current interval's target to an easy recovery pace, holding it until the workout naturally moves to the next step.
  • Skip step — jumps straight to the next step.
  • Auto-pause — if you stop pedaling (both cadence and power drop to near zero) for a few seconds, the ride pauses itself automatically, and resumes the moment you start pedaling again.
  • Stop — asks for confirmation first, then ends and saves the ride.

The Ramp Test

A structured warm-up followed by 1-minute steps rising in intensity until you can't hold on any more — stop it whenever you need to. It works out your best minute of power from the actual data and suggests a new FTP, clearly marked as an increase or decrease (with the percentage change) versus your current number, with a popup to accept or keep what you've got.

Strength sessions

Open TrainerStrength, pick a session (Full Body, Upper Body, Legs, Core, Push/Pull Day, or the hip/glute-focused Knee & Hip Support routine), and hit Start.

For each set, log the weight and the reps you did, then tap how many more you could have done (0 to 4+). Everything is filled in from last time, so most sets are one tap on Log set, and the rest timer starts when you log. Planks and other holds log seconds instead of reps. Use the dropdown to switch to the version you do, such as a goblet squat or a barbell back squat; each version keeps its own history.

Your weights are chosen for you. Each exercise has a rep range, such as 8–12. The first time you do one, the app starts from a light guess based on your body weight and lifting experience (Settings → Strength), then moves the next set up or down from how many reps you had left. After that, once every set reaches the top of the range with a rep or more to spare, the weight goes up to your next dumbbell and the reps start again from the bottom. Two sessions short of the range drops it about 10%, and after more than two weeks off it starts 10% lighter. If you had a hard ride in the last 24 hours, or your Form is very low, it holds the weight rather than going up. Body-weight moves suggest a harder version when they get easy, and holds go up 5 seconds at a time. The reason for each suggestion shows above the weight, and you can always change it.

Strength programmes. Under Programme on the TrainerStrength page, pick a multi-week block: Cyclist Strength (12 weeks: Build, Strength, then Maintain, with deloads in weeks 4 and 8), In-season Maintenance (one short, heavy session a week), Home Dumbbells, Bodyweight Start or Knee & Hip Support. Each phase changes the sets, rep range and rest. Sessions appear on Today and the Calendar. If you follow a training plan, they use its strength days first, then rest days, and avoid the day before a ride where they can. Without a plan, the programme is your plan.

Set the dumbbells, kettlebells and other kit you own in Settings → Strength. The weight buttons then step through only the weights you have. Your lifts on the TrainerStrength page shows every exercise you've logged with a chart, and your best sets appear under Records.

Strength around your riding. If a leg-heavy strength session lands the day before a hard ride, test, race, brick or fast run, Today suggests moving it to a nearby day that keeps your legs fresh. In a big riding week (well above your usual riding, or Form below -20), sessions start as a lighter version with 2 sets of each exercise; tap Do the full session to do it all, or turn this off in Settings → Strength. Leg-heavy sessions also count a little more towards your training load than upper-body or core sessions.

During a session. The rest timer beeps and vibrates for its last 3 seconds; tap Sound on to mute it, or +30 s for a longer rest. Swap exercise changes the current move to another version or a similar move for that session only, for example when the bench is taken. Skip exercise drops its remaining sets. Barbell lifts show the plates to load on each side of a 20 kg bar, and the first set of a heavy lift lists warm-up sets to do first (these aren't logged). If the page reloads or your phone closes it, open TrainerStrength again within 12 hours and it offers to pick up where you left off, with your logged sets intact.

When you finish, the session is saved to your history in Progress with every set, and ticks off the strength day in your plan or calendar. Stop part-way through and you can choose to save what you did. Strength sessions add a little to your training load: about 25 to 55 TSS an hour, more when your sets were close to the limit or worked your legs.

Reviewing rides

Every completed (or manually stopped) ride from Live Ride is saved automatically to Rides — no extra step needed. Each entry shows duration, average power, Normalized Power, Intensity Factor, and heart rate stats.

  • Click View chart on any ride to see power and heart rate plotted over the whole session, with the planned intensity zones colored in behind them.
  • Click Download TCX to download a file you can manually upload to Garmin Connect or Strava.
  • If you've connected Strava (in Settings), rides upload there automatically — no need to export and upload by hand.

Rides and runs brought in from Strava show their route on a map. Activities imported before maps were added get their routes over the next few Strava syncs.

Suggested rides

On days with nothing planned, Today suggests a ride from the library. It looks at:

  • How fresh you are: your Form, whether you rode hard yesterday, and how fast your Fitness is rising. Tired days get an easy ride; fresh days get something harder.
  • What you haven't done lately: the zones you haven't ridden for a while, and where your level is furthest behind.
  • Your levels: it aims for a workout that's Achievable or Productive for you.

Pick how long you want to ride and it picks a workout of that length, or within 5 minutes of it. If nothing in the library is that close, it shows the nearest length and says so. Tap Something else for a different kind of ride, and Tired or Fresh if you feel different from what your numbers say. On a plan day, Short on time? finds a shorter workout in the same zone that still ticks off the day.

The same suggestion is at the top of Workouts, and Live Ride has it ready when nothing else is planned.

Fitness, performance and records

Progress has four tabs:

  • Activities: everything you've done, from TrainerCycle, Strava or logged by hand.
  • Fitness: your Fitness, Fatigue and Form over 6 weeks to a year, with a dashed line showing where your plan takes you. Fitness is your average daily TSS over the last six weeks, Fatigue over the last week, and Form is Fitness minus Fatigue.
  • Performance: weekly hours or TSS by sport, your estimated FTP month by month, how much of your riding is easy or hard, run and swim pace trends, and how many planned sessions you've done.
  • Records: your best powers and times.

Your account

Settings is also where you manage your subscription — update your card, view invoices, or cancel, all through the Manage Subscription button. Something not working, or got a question? Use the Contact Support button right there too.

Tips & troubleshooting

  • Collapsed menu? Tap the ☰ icon top-left on any page to open the sidebar — that's where workout pickers and connection buttons live. FTP, trainer pairing, and Strava are all on the Settings page.
  • Nothing loading? Most pages will show the actual error message if a load fails, rather than just staying blank — if you see one, send it over and it points straight at the cause.
  • Signed out unexpectedly? Sign back in with the same email — a fresh magic link works every time, no password to remember.
  • Fullscreen icon (top-left, next to the menu) is handy for a clean, distraction-free view while mounted on the bike.
